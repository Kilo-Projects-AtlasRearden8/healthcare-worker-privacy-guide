Safe everyday habits

Small, repeatable habits matter more than perfect knowledge. Here are beginner-friendly ways to use AI without putting patient data at risk.

Before you start

  • Ask your privacy or compliance officer whether AI use is allowed at work
  • Use only tools your employer has approved for workplace tasks
  • Never log into a personal AI account on a work device unless allowed
  • Read the tool’s privacy policy — look for whether inputs are stored or used for training, and whether you can opt out or delete conversations

While using AI

  • Keep patient data out of prompts entirely. Use fake examples instead
  • Do not upload patient photos, scans, or documents
  • Turn off “memory” or “personalization” features at work if you can
  • Avoid sharing your own personal data too (home address, phone, financials)
  • Log out when you are done, especially on shared computers
  • Do not paste work credentials, passwords, or badge numbers

If your employer allows approved AI tools

  • Use the approved version, not the free public one
  • Follow the approved workflow exactly
  • Still treat the tool as if anything you type could be read by others
  • Document nothing about real patients in the tool unless your compliance officer has confirmed it is permitted

At home

  • AI is fine for personal learning, recipes, travel planning, and hobbies
  • Keep your own personal details light — you do not need to give an AI your birthdate to ask about medication side effects generally
  • Remember: education only. AI is not a substitute for your clinician

Daily checklist

A full printable version is in the checklist.

  • Before work AI use

    Privacy officer asked; approved tool only; privacy policy checked; logout known.

  • While using AI

    No patient identifiers, photos, documents, credentials, or staffing info. Fake examples only.

  • When finished

    Logged out. Tab closed, especially on shared computers.
