Patient data basics

You already know patient data deserves protection. This is a plain English refresher on what counts, why it matters, and where AI fits in.

What counts as patient data

Patient data is any information that can identify a patient and relates to their health, care, or payment. Common examples:

  • Names, dates of birth, addresses, phone numbers
  • Medical record numbers (MRNs), account numbers, patient IDs
  • Diagnoses, conditions, test results, medications, treatment plans
  • Photos, scans, voice recordings, wristband numbers
  • Insurance info, billing codes, appointment dates
  • Anything that, combined with other details, could identify someone

Even “de-identified” stories can be risky. Saying “a nurse on the night shift at County General” plus a diagnosis may be enough for someone to figure out who you mean. When in doubt, treat it as patient data.

Why AI chatbots are different from your EHR

Your electronic health record (EHR) is built for healthcare, with access logs, permissions, and audit trails. Most public AI chatbots are not:

  • What you paste may be stored by the AI provider
  • It may be used to train future models
  • Humans at the provider may read it
  • You usually cannot delete it fully or know exactly where it went
  • Once it leaves your control, you cannot take it back

Using an AI chatbot is not the same as documenting in the EHR. Assume anything you paste could become public.

HIPAA in one paragraph

HIPAA (the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act) sets rules for protecting patients’ protected health information (PHI). Covered entities and their business associates must follow them. Violations can mean fines, investigation, and job loss. Your employer’s policies are often stricter than HIPAA — follow the stricter rule.

This manual is not legal advice. Ask your compliance or privacy officer about your workplace’s rules.

Your workplace comes first

  1. Ask your privacy or compliance officer if AI use is allowed
  2. Check whether your employer has an approved AI tool
  3. Never paste patient data into a tool your employer has not vetted
  4. Follow your employer’s policy, even if this guide says something different
